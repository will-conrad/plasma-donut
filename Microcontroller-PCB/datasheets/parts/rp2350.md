## How it's used on this board

- The silicon inside the Pico 2. This datasheet has the GPIO electrical limits, the PWM edge-counter used to measure frequency, the SPI peripheral and the errata.

## Gotchas and design notes

- **Erratum E9 (p.1359):** an input pad that sits between VIL and VIH leaks about 120 µA and floats to about 2.2 V. Internal pull-downs can't overcome it, but internal pull-ups work. Pulling the pin down needs 8.2 kΩ or less. So: buttons are active-low with pull-ups, and any slow or analog-ish signal on a GPIO needs a low-impedance driver.
- **Frequency counting (p.980):** only PWM **B** pins (odd GPIOs) can count edges. Both the high and low halves of the input must be longer than one clk_sys period. 13.56 MHz has a 36.9 ns half-period, against 6.7 ns for clk_sys at 150 MHz, so it's fine.
- **PWM slice sharing (p.976):** GPIO *n* and GPIO *n*+16 drive the same PWM channel. If two frequency inputs share a slice's B input, the counter sees the OR of the two signals. Put each frequency input on its own slice.
- Logic levels at 3.3 V (p.1337): VIH ≥ 2.0 V, VIL ≤ 0.8 V, VOH ≥ 2.62 V at the rated drive current.
- All GPIOs together can source at most 100 mA and sink at most 100 mA (p.1338).
